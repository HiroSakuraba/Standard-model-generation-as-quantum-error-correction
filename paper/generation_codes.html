Beyond One Generation

Family-Coupled Quantum Codes from Standard Model Charges

Benjamin John Schulz

October 2026

Abstract

Prakash and Ramya observed that cubic anomaly cancellation for one Standard Model generation, completed by a right-handed neutrino, is the triorthogonality condition that defines a [[15,1,3]]p quantum code with a transversal non-Clifford gate. We ask what happens when the three generations are combined. Because the generations carry identical gauge charges, stacking them yields one logical qudit, and a family-number charge yields distance two. Family-coupled charge lattices give [[30,2,3]]p and [[45,3,3]]p triorthogonal codes; one such lattice uses only charges that commute with SU(3)×SU(2), namely family-dependent B−LB-L and right-handed isospin, and works for every prime p≥5p\geq 5. We prove that no new charge is compatible with a single generation over any odd prime, and that every lattice built from family-wise Cartan charges has distance at most three. Exact logical-channel enumerators show that coupling leaves the leading distillation error unchanged but produces correlated two-output errors at order ε4\varepsilon^4, where independent codes first produce them at ε6\varepsilon^6; we derive the routing rule that keeps these correlations out of a second distillation round. The coupled codes are not equivalent to any tensor product of smaller codes under local unitaries and qudit permutations. Nature does not supply the required charges: with one Higgs doublet, full-rank quark masses and non-block-diagonal mixing force every such family charge to be family-universal, and the charges needed for distance three act on lepton doublets, which neutrino mixing breaks at order one. The same structure explains, in coding language, why E8E_8 embeddings fail: anomaly cancellation by mirror fermions reduces the code distance to one.

1. Introduction

Quantum error correction has become a useful language for fundamental physics. Holographic codes made precise the sense in which a bulk region of anti-de Sitter space is encoded redundantly in its boundary [1, 2]. The Hayden–Preskill analysis of black-hole information is, at heart, a statement about how fast a scrambled code can be decoded [3]. In the other direction, Kitaev’s toric code is a ℤ2\mathbb Z_2 lattice gauge theory, and its protection comes from the structure of gauge constraints [4, 5].

Prakash and Ramya have added a new and unexpected entry to this dictionary [6]. Take one generation of Standard Model fermions together with a right-handed neutrino, sixteen Weyl fields in all, and arrange their charges under the Cartan generators of SO(10) as a matrix. Cubic anomaly cancellation says that the entrywise product of any three rows sums to zero. In coding theory this is triorthogonality, the property that lets a CSS code implement a non-Clifford gate transversally [7]. Puncturing the right-handed neutrino produces a [[15,1,3]]p code for every prime pp, the qudit analogue of the 15-qubit Reed–Muller code used in magic-state distillation [8, 9].

A single generation is only part of the story. Nature repeats the generation three times, and the repetition is one of the oldest unexplained features of the Standard Model. This paper asks whether the three generations, taken together, define a larger code, and what that code can or cannot do. The answer has three parts.

  1. Constructions. With suitable family-dependent charges the generations couple into [[30,2,3]]p and [[45,3,3]]p triorthogonal codes. One version uses only charges that commute with the Standard Model gauge group.
  2. Limits. A single generation admits no further charges (Theorem 1), and no lattice of this type exceeds distance three (Theorem 2). The coupling produces correlated logical errors but no new transversal gates.
  3. Physics. The family charges that make the construction work are precisely those that the observed masses and mixings destroy (Proposition 3). The obstruction has the same structure as the one that defeats E8E_8 unification.

All results are exact. Codes are computed in finite-field arithmetic, anomaly sums are checked over the integers, and every number quoted here is asserted by a script in the accompanying repository (Appendix A).

Outline. Section 2 places the work in context. Section 3 sets up the correspondence between charge lattices and codes. Section 4 describes the obstruction to combining generations, and Section 5 resolves it. Section 6 restricts the construction to gauge-compatible charges. Section 7 proves the limits. Section 8 computes the logical channels and shows the coupled codes are genuinely new. Section 9 asks whether nature supplies the required charges. Section 10 summarizes. 2. Context

Codes and fundamental physics. Error-correcting structure has appeared in quantum gravity [1, 2, 3] and in lattice gauge theory, where Gauss-law constraints have been used directly for error detection [10]. The present work differs in its object. The code here is not built from a spacetime geometry or a Hilbert space of gauge fields; it is read off from the charge table of the matter content.

Anomalies and transversal gates. A growing literature connects transversal non-Clifford gates to symmetry-protected topological phases and to higher-group symmetries and their anomalies in gauge theories [11, 12, 13]. The Prakash–Ramya correspondence is a direct, elementary instance of the same theme: the cubic anomaly is exactly what makes the transversal phase gate act logically (Section 3.3). The codes considered here are not geometrically local, so the Bravyi–König restrictions on transversal gates in local codes [14] do not apply.

Triorthogonal and qudit distillation codes. Triorthogonal codes were introduced by Bravyi and Haah [7]; small qubit examples are classified in [15], with an extended classification through length 54 in [16]. Prime-dimension distillation was developed in [9] and refined in [17, 18, 19]; cyclic triorthogonal codes in prime dimension are treated in [20]. We compare our codes with the published qudit families in Section 8.3.

Flavour. Hierarchical Yukawa couplings from broken family symmetries go back to Froggatt and Nielsen [21]; family-dependent gauged hypercharge has been proposed as an origin of flavour [22]. Section 9 uses these ideas to ask how badly the charges our codes need are broken.

E8E_8. Lisi’s proposal places one generation inside E8E_8 [23]; Distler and Garibaldi proved that no such embedding of gravity and the Standard Model is chiral [24]. Section 9.4 translates their obstruction into a statement about code distance.

3.3 Where the anomaly enters

Apply D=ωx3D=\omega^{x^3} to every physical qudit, p≥5p\geq5. On |aGP⟩|aG_P\rangle it produces the phase ωΦ(a)\omega^{\Phi(a)} with Φ(a)=∑j∈P(a⋅hj)3\Phi(a)=\sum_{j\in P}(a\cdot h_j)^3. Expanding the cube gives the triple sums ∑jhijhkjhlj\sum_j h_{ij}h_{kj}h_{lj}. Cubic anomaly cancellation states that these vanish when summed over all sixteen fields, so (4)∑j∈P(a⋅hj)3=−∑j∈R(a⋅hj)3=−∑ici3. \sum_{j\in P}(a\cdot h_j)^3 \;=\; -\sum_{j\in R}(a\cdot h_j)^3 \;=\; -\sum_i c_i^3 . The phase depends only on the logical label, and the transversal gate acts as D−1D^{-1} on each logical qudit. The anomaly is precisely the statement that the physical phases assemble into a logical one. For p=3p=3 the same argument holds with ninth roots of unity because the cancellation is exact over the integers [6].

For one generation, 𝒞\mathcal C is a [[15,1,3]]p code for every odd prime, with 4 independent X checks, 10 Z checks, X distance 7, and exactly 25 three-site supports of nontrivial ZZ logicals.

4. The twins problem

The second generation alone gives the same code. Mass does not enter the charge table, and the muon family’s columns coincide with the electron family’s. To obtain two logical qudits we place two generations side by side: 32 columns, two punctured neutrinos, 30 physical qudits. Each field then has a twin in the other family with an identical column, and so do the two logical slots.

Two simple choices fail. With shared charges only, the punctured columns coincide and k=1k=1: the code is [[30,1,3]]p. With an added family-number charge, +1+1 on one family and −1-1 on the other, the punctured columns separate and k=2k=2. But a twin pair carrying ZZ and Z−1Z^{-1} has charge (0,…,0,2)(0,\dots,0,2), which is exactly the difference of the logical columns, so every twin pair is a weight-two logical operator and the code is [[30,2,2]]p.

The general statement rests on a property of the weights.

Lemma 1. For pp odd, q0q_0 is neither a multiple of one non-singlet weight nor a linear combination of two.

Consider a two-family lattice in which field qq of family s=±1s=\pm1 has column (q,sBq)(q,\,sBq), with BB an integer m×5m\times 5 matrix. By Lemma 1, a nontrivial ZZ logical of weight at most two must cancel in the shared rows, which forces a twin pair with opposite coefficients. Its charge is (0,2Bq)(0,2Bq), while the logical columns differ by (0,2Bq0)(0,2Bq_0). This proves:

(Distance at least three follows from the lemma; distance exactly three follows from Theorem 2 below.) A family-number charge fails because it is the same for every field. The extra charges must depend on the field, not only on the family.

7. Limits

7.1 Rigidity of a single generation

Because q5=q1q2q3q4q_5=q_1q_2q_3q_4, the set 𝒬\mathcal Q is the group (ℤ2)4(\mathbb Z_2)^4. 7.3 Two further observations

Apparent coupling can be illusory. Consider nonlinear differential rows (χs,−χs)(\chi_s,-\chi_s). The triorthogonality conditions reduce to sa⊕sb∉As_a\oplus s_b\notin A, and protecting every twin pair requires five rows. Exactly sixteen sets qualify, {a⊕t:a∈A}\{a\oplus t:a\in A\} for t∈𝔽24t\in\mathbb F_2^4. Each is two independent copies of the one-generation code after a change of variables: replacing shared and differential rows by their half-sum and half-difference and exchanging each field with its twin whenever χt(q)=−1\chi_t(q)=-1 yields a direct sum. Nevertheless, 44 of the 50 minimum-weight logical supports cross the original family boundary. Cross-family support counts therefore do not establish coupling; Section 8.2 gives an invariant that does.

Rows that cancel only modulo pp. If one allows a row that is triorthogonal modulo pp but not over the integers, as for a discrete ℤp\mathbb Z_p charge whose anomaly conditions hold only modulo pp [25], new options appear. Extending B5B_5, there are none at p=5p=5 or p=11p=11; there are 588 rows in 17 orbits at p=7p=7 and 4,056 rows in 57 orbits at p=13p=13. All give distance three, but with 8 rather than 50 three-site logical supports. At p=7p=7 the leading distillation error falls from 2518ε3\tfrac{25}{18}\varepsilon^3 to 29ε3\tfrac{2}{9}\varepsilon^3, a factor of 6.25, and the X distance is 9. These rows are not invariant under the gauge group and do not correspond to consistent U(1)U(1) charges.

8.2 Correlated outputs in a distillation factory

Fault-tolerance thresholds are usually proven under assumptions that limit correlations among faults [26, 27]. The correlations found here are of a different kind. They are not correlations in the physical noise; the code itself produces them in its outputs. Whether they matter depends on how outputs are consumed.

Consider two rounds of distillation in which each first-round output has error probability δ≈cε3\delta\approx c\,\varepsilon^3 and the second round uses a code with distance three. If the inputs to a second-round block are independent, its leading failure probability is O(δ3)=O(ε9)O(\delta^3)=O(\varepsilon^9). Suppose instead that two of its inputs come from the same coupled first-round block and occupy two sites of a three-site logical support of the second-round code. Both are wrong with probability O(ε4)O(\varepsilon^4), and one further error on the third site, with probability O(ε3)O(\varepsilon^3), completes the logical. The failure probability becomes O(ε7)O(\varepsilon^7), and two orders of suppression are lost.

The remedy is a routing rule: outputs of the same coupled block must feed different second-round blocks. With that rule the correlations do not enter at leading order, and the coupled codes behave like independent copies in a two-round protocol. A circuit-level analysis with noisy checks of weight 15 and 16 would be needed to settle the overhead, and we have not done it.

The coupled codes are also more expensive to measure. At p=5p=5 a minimum-weight check basis has X checks of weight 15 and 16 against 8 for independent copies, for a total check weight of 170 against 144.

8.3 The coupled codes are new

Section 7.3 showed that a code can appear coupled while being a direct sum. The following criterion is invariant under any local change of basis.

Proposition 2. A stabilizer code with stabilizer group SS factorizes as 𝒞A⊗𝒞B\mathcal C_A\otimes\mathcal C_B across a partition A|BA|B of its qudits if and only if S=SA×SBS=S_A\times S_B, where SAS_A denotes the subgroup supported on AA.

Proof. The code projector is Π=|S|−1∑s∈Ss\Pi=|S|^{-1}\sum_{s\in S}s. If Π=ΠA⊗ΠB\Pi=\Pi_A\otimes\Pi_B, its Pauli expansion is supported on a product set TA×TBT_A\times T_B. The identity lies in TBT_B, so (tA,1)∈S(t_A,1)\in S for all tA∈TAt_A\in T_A, and S=SA×SBS=S_A\times S_B. The converse is immediate. ▫\square

Factorizability across some partition is preserved by local unitaries and qudit permutations. For a CSS code, the partitions across which SS splits are unions of connected components of two linear matroids, the X-check space and the row space of GPG_P, and these components can be computed exactly. Every coupled code in Table 2, for two and three families at every prime tested, has a single component; a direct sum has one component per block.

Corollary. No coupled code in Table 2 is equivalent, under local unitaries and qudit permutations, to independent one-generation codes or to any tensor product of smaller codes.

An independent invariant gives the same conclusion. For four qudits AA the reduced code state has purity TrρA2=pdimSA−4\operatorname{Tr}\rho_A^2=p^{\dim S_A-4}. The number of four-qudit sets with enhanced purity is 150 for two independent copies, against 184 (p=3p=3) and 249 (p=5p=5) for the coupled codes; for three families it is 225 against 327 and 522.

We have also excluded two published qudit families. The Prakash–Saha qutrit codes [[9m−k,k,2]]3 [18] have distance two and cannot match (n,k)=(30,2)(n,k)=(30,2) or (45,3)(45,3). Full-evaluation punctured Reed–Muller codes [19] require n+k=pmn+k=p^m, whereas here n+kn+k is 32 or 48. A systematic comparison with all known qudit triorthogonal codes remains to be done.

8.4 No new transversal gates

Consider all transversal diagonal gates ⨂jDwj\bigotimes_jD^{w_j} with D=ωx3D=\omega^{x^3} or ωx2\omega^{x^2} and arbitrary powers wj∈𝔽pw_j\in\mathbb F_p, p≥5p\geq5. Such a gate preserves the code if and only if (11)∑jwj(x⋅hj)(y⋅hj)(σ⋅hj)=0for all x,y∈𝔽pr and all σ with σGR=0, \sum_jw_j\,(x\cdot h_j)(y\cdot h_j)(\sigma\cdot h_j)=0\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb F_p^r\text{ and all }\sigma\text{ with }\sigma G_R=0, or the corresponding quadratic condition. The constraint is linear in ww, so the full space of such gates is computable. The induced logical gate is the restriction of a cubic or quadratic form to logical labels, and it is entangling exactly when that form has mixed terms. For every code considered here (coupled, gauge-compatible, mod-7, direct sum, and family number) the mixed part vanishes. The coupling is visible in the errors and invisible in the gates. Non-diagonal and permutation-assisted transversal gates are not covered by this analysis.

9. Does nature supply the charges?

9.1 Exact family charges and the quark masses

Proposition 3. Consider G≥2G\geq2 quark families, one Higgs doublet, and an unbroken family charge in the gauge-compatible span, aiS3+biS2a_iS_3+b_iS_2 on family ii. If the up and down mass matrices have full rank and the quark mixing matrix admits no nontrivial common block decomposition, then aia_i and bib_i are independent of ii.

Proof. The doublet QiQ_i carries charge aia_i, and ujcu^c_j, djcd^c_j carry −aj+2bj-a_j+2b_j and −aj−2bj-a_j-2b_j. An up-type Yukawa entry (i,j)(i,j) is allowed only if ai−aj+2bj+ηu=0a_i-a_j+2b_j+\eta_u=0, where ηu\eta_u is the Higgs charge. Invariance implies that YuYu†Y_uY_u^\dagger and YdYd†Y_dY_d^\dagger commute with the charge on the doublets. Distinct aia_i would then make both block diagonal in a common basis, and the mixing matrix with them; hence all aia_i are equal. Each column jj of YuY_u is then entirely allowed or entirely forbidden according to 2bj+ηu2b_j+\eta_u. Full rank forbids a zero column, and 2 is invertible, so all bjb_j are equal. ▫\square

A deterministic enumeration of every charge assignment modulo pp, for G=2G=2 at p=3,5,7p=3,5,7 and G=3G=3 at p=3,5p=3,5, confirms that exactly p2p^2 assignments survive, all family-universal. Family-universal charges make the punctured columns coincide, so k=1k=1. With exact family charges, three generations encode one logical qudit.

The proposition is the familiar statement that generic Yukawa couplings leave no family-dependent abelian symmetry. Its content here is the consequence for the code. It concerns unbroken symmetries in the one-Higgs quark sector; models with several Higgs doublets, spurions, or non-abelian flavour groups require separate treatment.

9.2 How badly are the charges broken?

Exact invariance is excluded, so the relevant question is the size of the breaking, in the Froggatt–Nielsen sense [21]. A family charge acting on the doublets forces the mixing matrix to be block diagonal; its breaking is bounded below by the largest mixing element that no choice of basis removes.

A charge acting only on right-handed fields is unaffected by mixing. With one Higgs doublet it allows one Yukawa column per sector, and its breaking is set by mass ratios: mc/mt=0.004m_c/m_t=0.004, ms/mb=0.019m_s/m_b=0.019 and mμ/mτ=0.059m_\mu/m_\tau=0.059 at MZM_Z [30].

These numbers decide the question. The right-handed charge S2S_2 is broken only at the percent level, but by Section 6 it gives distance two. Distance three requires S3S_3 as well, and S3S_3 acts on the lepton doublets with value −3-3, where neutrino mixing breaks it at order one. No distance-three code in this family is protected by a small symmetry-breaking parameter in the observed flavour sector.

9.3 Superselection

A second obstruction is independent of flavour. In the conjugate basis of Section 3.2, logical states lie in different sectors of total charge. For an exact gauge charge, total charge is fixed by the flux at infinity, and coherent superpositions of different total charge cannot be prepared or detected with local operations [31]. Such superpositions acquire operational meaning only relative to a charged reference system [32, 33]. A logical qudit of this code would then be stored in the relation between the matter and the reference, not in the matter alone. The codes describe the charge table; they are implemented on engineered hardware, not by elementary particles.

9.4 E8E_8

Under E8⊃SO(10)×SO(6)E_8\supset SO(10)\times SO(6), the 128 spinorial roots of E8E_8 decompose as (12)𝟏𝟐𝟖=(𝟏𝟔,𝟒)⊕(𝟏𝟔¯,𝟒‾), \mathbf{128}=(\mathbf{16},\mathbf 4)\oplus(\overline{\mathbf{16}},\bar{\mathbf 4}), four copies of a generation labelled by SU(4) weights, together with their mirrors. Distler and Garibaldi showed that embeddings of this kind cannot be chiral [24]. In the present language:

  • The chiral block (𝟏𝟔,𝟒)(\mathbf{16},\mathbf 4) is not triorthogonal. The triple sum over the three SU(4) Cartan rows equals 64, which fails over the integers and modulo 3, 5 and 7.
  • The full 𝟏𝟐𝟖\mathbf{128} is triorthogonal because every column appears with its negative. Each punctured column’s mirror is then a physical column and a weight-one logical operator, and the code is [[124,4,1]]p.
  • Using the SU(4) weights as family labels on the chiral block gives the family-number construction, [[60,4,2]]p. Cartan charges of a product group act additively, (x,y)⋅(q,w)=x⋅q+y⋅w(x,y)\cdot(q,w)=x\cdot q+y\cdot w, and cannot produce the products fg(b⋅q)f_g\,(b\cdot q) that distance three requires.

E8E_8 cancels anomalies in the only way available to it, by pairing each field with its mirror, and in coding terms that pairing removes the protection.

10. Discussion

The argument can be summarized in one chain. Anomaly cancellation is triorthogonality, so a single generation is a magic-state code. The other generations give the same code because the charge table does not see mass. Combining them requires charges that distinguish the families field by field; the gauge group permits exactly two such charges per family, and with both the families couple into [[30,2,3]]p and [[45,3,3]]p codes for p≥5p\geq5. A single generation admits nothing further, distance three is a ceiling, and the coupling appears in correlated errors rather than in gates. The observed masses and mixings then remove every exact family charge, and the charge that distance three requires is broken at order one by neutrino mixing.

The result says something about the generations themselves. At the level of gauge charges the three generations are indistinguishable. At the level of masses, where particles propagate, they are mixed. A multi-family code requires a sharp and conserved answer to the question “which family is this?”, and neither level provides one. The E8E_8 case reaches the same conclusion from the opposite direction: a large symmetry group cannot supply families that are both chiral and sharply labelled.

We list the open problems.

  1. Compare the coupled codes systematically with known qudit triorthogonal codes, using canonical forms rather than invariants.
  2. Extend the gate analysis to non-diagonal and permutation-assisted transversal gates, and to the ninth-root gates at p=3p=3.
  3. Explain why the nonlinear rows of Section 7.3 appear only for p≡1(mod3)p\equiv1\pmod 3, and whether the floor of eight weight-three supports can be lowered.
  4. Carry out a circuit-level analysis of a two-round factory, with noisy checks and the routing rule of Section 8.2.
  5. Determine whether multi-Higgs or non-abelian flavour models can protect a family charge that acts on lepton doublets at a useful level.
