# SU(2) exchange-only composite pulse: calibrated robustness versus gate duration

**Status — 18 October 2026:** deterministic numerical simulation of an **SK1-inspired composite control sequence** on the *same* eight-spin SU(2)-invariant mediator Hamiltonian as PRs #15–16. The local axes are constructed from real, co-located Heisenberg exchanges. The result is **not a hardware-native complete fault-tolerant gate**, nor a mechanism established for the Standard Model. Executable: [\`su2_sk1_composite_pulse.py\`](su2_sk1_composite_pulse.py).

## 1. Why a new control axis is necessary

The preceding [smooth-pulse study](SU2_SMOOTH_PULSE_OPTIMIZATION.md) produced a nominal CZ-class operation with leakage below \(10^{-7}\), but a \(+10\%\) calibration error in **one of the two SU(2)-scalar cross-register exchanges** changed the encoded gate by about \(0.088\) in operator norm. That fault leaves all gauge laws satisfied: measuring Gauss charge cannot identify it.

In the second-order model, the entangling coupling is \(J_{ZZ}=-J_1J_2/(24\Delta)\). Multiplying a single cross coupling by \(1+\epsilon\) therefore multiplies the \(ZZ\) phase by \(1+\epsilon\) at leading order. Simply repeating commuting \(ZZ\) rotations at positive durations **cannot independently cancel that first derivative** while producing a nonzero desired phase, since both desired and erroneous phases add.

A known solution in quantum control is to introduce **noncommuting rotation axes**, then arrange corrective rotations whose first-order error generators cancel. This is the principle of SK1-style composite pulses, not an original invention here. Relevant primary literature:

- Brown, Harrow and Chuang, [*Arbitrarily accurate composite pulse sequences*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.70.052318), *Physical Review A* **70**, 052318 (2004), and [its 2005 erratum](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.72.039905).
- [*Improving the gate fidelity of capacitively coupled spin qubits*](https://doi.org/10.1038/npjqi20153), *npj Quantum Information* (2015), develops a related exchange-coupled spin-qubit two-body SK1 generalization. Its model is not the present matter/rishon Hamiltonian.

## 2. Microscopic allowed operators

The code contains two logical qubits: an SU(2) four-spin-singlet encoded ancilla \(A\) and an SU(2) four-spin-singlet encoded data register \(D\). Each consists of four spin-\(\tfrac12\) SU(2) fundamentals at a **single gauge vertex**. Four codewords live inside the global spin-zero sector of the **full 256-dimensional eight-spin Hilbert space**.

The pulsed interaction is unchanged:

\[
H(t)=\Delta(\mathbf S_A^2+\mathbf S_D^2)
+J_1(t)\,\mathbf S_{A_0}\cdot\mathbf S_{D_0}
+J_2(t)\,\mathbf S_{A_1}\cdot\mathbf S_{D_1},
\]

where \(J_1=J_2=\Delta\sin^2(\pi t/T)\) for each smoothly switched primitive. All elementary Hamiltonian terms are **pairwise SU(2)-invariant Heisenberg exchanges**, plus the block Casimirs that themselves consist of these pairwise exchanges.

Within each **individual** four-spin singlet block, the logical Pauli operators are generated by *same-block* exchange combinations:

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z_A&=-\mathrm{SWAP}_{A_0 A_1},&
X_A&=\frac{\mathrm{SWAP}_{A_0 A_3}-\mathrm{SWAP}_{A_0 A_2}}{\sqrt3},\\
Z_D&=-\mathrm{SWAP}_{D_0 D_1},&
X_D&=\frac{\mathrm{SWAP}_{D_0 D_3}-\mathrm{SWAP}_{D_0 D_2}}{\sqrt3}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The code verifies these operators' **full action on the four singlet codewords**, and verifies that they preserve the entire 14D global SU(2) spin-zero sector. Since each is a color scalar, the logical axis changes need **no gauge-violating local spin flips**.

**Important hardware assumption:** the single-block rotations \(R_{X_A}\), \(R_{X_D}\), \(R_{Z_D}\) used between entangling pulses are treated as **perfect and instantaneous applications of the specified physical, pairwise-exchange Hamiltonians**. Their finite pulse durations, switch settling, control errors, and dynamic effects while leaked amplitude occupies excited states are not included in the gate-time tally or robustness study. They are additional physical control requirements, not free logical gates.

## 3. Echo plus SK1-inspired correction

Let \(U(t)\) denote the full-physics evolution under one smooth simultaneous cross-exchange pulse of duration \(t\), at \(J_{\max}/\Delta=1\). Define the *physical exchange echo*

\[
L=R_{X_A}(\pi)R_{X_D}(\pi),\qquad
E(t)=L\,U(t)\,L\,U(t).
\]

The echo cancels the single-qubit \(Z_A\) and \(Z_D\) phases in the logical approximation while retaining \(Z_AZ_D\) entanglement. The full pulse does cause small finite leakage, which is **computed rather than set to zero**.

Define the unwrapped nonlocal phase

\[
\Theta(t)=\arg W_{00}+\arg W_{11}-\arg W_{01}-\arg W_{10},
\quad W=Q^\dagger E(t)Q,
\]

with the branch chosen continuously from zero phase at zero time. Calibrate two durations at nominal couplings:

\[
\Theta(t_{\rm target})=\pi,\qquad
\Theta(t_{\rm correction})=4\pi.
\]

The first is locally equivalent to CZ; the second implements a nominal \(2\pi\) encoded \(ZZ\) rotation, i.e. a logically trivial correction pulse up to overall phase.

Tilt the corrective rotation axes using logical *data-register* \(Y_D\) rotations synthesized from \(X_D\) and \(Z_D\) exchanges:

\[
R_{Y_D}(\phi)=R_{Z_D}(\pi/2)R_{X_D}(\phi)R_{Z_D}(-\pi/2).
\]

Choose \(\phi=\arccos(-1/8)\). With \(P=R_{Y_D}(\phi)\) and \(M=R_{Y_D}(-\phi)\), construct the **SK1-inspired** sequence

\[
\boxed{
U_{\rm comp}
=M E(t_{\rm correction})M^\dagger\,
 P E(t_{\rm correction})P^\dagger\,
 E(t_{\rm target}).
}
\]

The two conjugated \(2\pi\) corrective rotations are identity up to phase at the *ideal logical gate* level, but their **opposing noncommuting error generators** cancel the leading amplitude error of the target. The first-order cancellation is exact for the ideal rotation model; for the true eight-spin pulse it is **approximate and assessed numerically**. The script checks both independent coupling errors rather than presuming they combine identically.

The calibrated duration is approximately

\[
t_{\rm target}\Delta\simeq19.07,\qquad
t_{\rm correction}\Delta\simeq76.52,
\]

giving **six smooth exchange envelopes** and total cross-exchange-active time

\[
\boxed{T_{\rm active}\Delta=
2(t_{\rm target}+2t_{\rm correction})\simeq344.24}.
\]

This is roughly **nine times** the single shaped gate duration \(38.23/\Delta\) from PR #16, **before** counting the finite duration of the interleaved SU(2)-invariant local rotations. It is a significant cost that must be included in any physical feasibility comparison.

## 4. Exhaustive deterministic calibration stress: both physical couplers

This experiment applies the same fixed pulse schedule under coherent multiplicative calibration faults \(J_j(t)\to(1+\epsilon_j)J_j(t)\). Each faulted result is compared with its own **fixed nominal sequence**, not a *fault-dependently recalibrated* logical CZ. Only an overall physically irrelevant complex phase is fitted. For the compressed logical operation \(W=Q^\dagger UQ\),

\[
\delta(U,U_0)=\min_{\alpha}
\|e^{i\alpha}W-W_0\|_2,
\]

implemented with a trace-phase alignment. The use of this specific phase alignment means the reported value is **not mathematically certified to be the minimizing \(\alpha\)**; it is a transparent reproducible deviation from a fixed nominal gate. The maximum code leakage is \(1-\lambda_{\min}(W^\dagger W)\), as in PRs #15–16.

The full time-ordered 14D global-singlet evolution, exactly embedded into 256D, includes all six smooth pulses and all ideal SU(2)-scalar local rotations. The pulse times are calibrated on a **512-bin** midpoint discretization, with **256- and 1,024-bin** checks carried out afterward to guard against timestep artifacts.

Representative deviations (one physical cross-coupler in error; the other nominal):

| Coupler amplitude error | Single echoed gate deviation | Composite gate deviation | Improvement |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(-10\%\) | 0.08636 | **0.02920** | \(\approx2.96\times\) |
| \(-5\%\) | 0.04338 | **0.00744** | \(\approx5.83\times\) |
| \(-1\%\) | 0.00871 | **0.00030** | \(\approx29\times\) |
| \(+1\%\) | 0.00872 | **0.00030** | \(\approx29\times\) |
| \(+5\%\) | 0.04374 | **0.00757** | \(\approx5.78\times\) |
| \(+10\%\) | 0.08782 | **0.03019** | \(\approx2.91\times\) |

A fault in **either physical cross coupler** yields the same result up to numerical symmetry. Joint errors in both couplers are tested separately; at \(\pm10\%\) jointly the reduction is less pronounced (roughly \(0.163\to0.101\) for \(-10\%\), \(0.185\to0.128\) for \(+10\%\)). These deterministic operator distances are **not stochastic logical error rates, average gate infidelities, or proven threshold data**.

The composite's nominal final code leakage is approximately \(1.7\times10^{-6}\), and the tested one-coupler fault cases remain below \(2\times10^{-6}\), **worse nominal leakage than the \(9.8\times10^{-8}\) single shaped-gate result from PR #16**. The new sequence improves one error mechanism—coupling calibration sensitivity—while worsening duration and somewhat increasing leakage. Under substantial gate-duration-dependent decoherence it might perform **worse** than the short gate.

### Falsification test: errors in the *extra* local exchange rotations

The new composite pulses rely on exact intra-register \(X\) and \(Z\) exchange rotations both for echo flips and for tilting the corrective axes. A second stress test scales **all** such local rotation angles by the same factor \(1+\epsilon_{\rm local}\) while leaving both physical cross-coupler envelopes nominal.

| Common intra-register exchange rotation calibration error | Logical operator deviation from nominal | Final code leakage |
|---:|---:|---:|
| \(-5\%\) | **0.481473** | \(1.73\times10^{-6}\) |
| \(-1\%\) | **0.099783** | \(1.73\times10^{-6}\) |
| \(+1\%\) | **0.101317** | \(1.73\times10^{-6}\) |
| \(+5\%\) | **0.517885** | \(1.75\times10^{-6}\) |

These results are **far worse** than the cross-coupler calibration deviations the SK1 sequence was designed to suppress. The test is a coherent simultaneous amplitude fault in every extra local exchange operation; it is not a measurement of actual device error rates. The local pulses are still treated as zero-duration ideal rotations *except for their miscalibrated angle*.

**Research conclusion:** the composite is demonstrably *not* calibration-robust as a full physical control protocol. Its corrective rotations have become the dominant modeled vulnerability. Future optimization must include both sets of physical controls simultaneously; improving the cross-coupler error alone would be misleading.

### Convergence and full-spin audit

At a \({+10\%}\) first-coupler fault, the composite deviation is approximately:

| Midpoint bins per primitive | Faulted-versus-nominal deviation |
|---:|---:|
| 128 | 0.030186 |
| 256 | 0.030224 |
| 512 | 0.030233 |
| 1,024 | **0.030235** |

Calibration on 512 bins and retesting at higher resolution avoids treating an accidentally tiny coarse-grid score as a physical theorem. The program also independently propagates **all four physical code states through the complete six-pulse composite sequence in the full 256D Hilbert space** at a 12-bin audit grid using sparse matrix exponentials, including the physical local exchange rotations; it compares that result against the separately computed 14D SU(2)-singlet reduction. Numerical agreement certifies **consistency of the symmetry reduction**, not a continuum-time bound on pulse error.

## 5. What the result does and does not demonstrate

**Achieved:** an explicit **two-body-exchange-only**, noncommuting, SU(2)-gauge-invariant composite-control strategy; all local rotations and entangling pulses are constructed from specified physical spin operators. In the truncated eight-spin model it reduces small, independent, coherent cross-coupler miscalibration errors from **linear** to approximately **quadratic** scaling at the operator level, with full code leakage and duration exposed.

**Not achieved:** calibration robustness of the full sequence (a mere 1% local-exchange rotation error causes ~0.10 operator deviation), low-overhead robust control, optimized local rotation synthesis, physical bandwidth/noise modeling, reliability under random/temporally changing calibration drifts, realistic ancilla preparation/readout, explicit leakage detection, or a complete five-module error-correction circuit compiled from these pulses. Importantly, the logical-rotation model treats physical local exchange pulses as *instantaneous and error-free*; correcting this approximation is the next essential check.

**Next research release criteria:**

1. Incorporate **finite-duration, miscalibrated** intra-block X/Z rotations and always-on block Casimir dynamics into the composite simulation; compare both nominal performance and fault sensitivity.
2. Optimize gate time/leakage/robustness jointly, including pulse bandwidth and oscillator/idle noise, rather than minimizing a single deterministic operator norm.
3. Compile a **single measured outer stabilizer** with this physically generated entangler and full ancilla-singlet preparation/readout, then test whether one physical exchange fault propagates to two data modules.
4. Only after demonstrating compatible physical encoding/recovery should this work be used to infer anything about the original anomaly-derived Standard Model charge code.
