# SU(2) singlet memory: off-graph exchange robustness and explicit recovery

**18 October 2026 — reproducible finite-model experiment.** Companion executable: [`su2_offgraph_robustness.py`](su2_offgraph_robustness.py). Exact exchange algebra/Knill–Laflamme identities use SymPy 1.14; the 256-dimensional time-evolution and recovery benchmark uses NumPy/SciPy double precision. The result is *not* a new code family, Standard Model particle encoding, physical device prediction, or fault-tolerant threshold.

## 1. Question and physical interpretation

Prior work proved a star-shaped exchange graph can protect one encoded singlet qubit, while a single additional nonstar bridge can eliminate that exact *full-algebra* protection. Here we distinguish three questions:

1. **Exact algebra:** What happens to the error algebra if any off-graph exchange is admitted as an independently allowed error operator?
2. **Finite-amplitude channel:** How much does a weak off-graph coupling actually perturb the stored information over a specified finite interval?
3. **Recovery:** Can the information be recovered even after substantial apparent leakage from the original code *subspace*?

Model: eight spin-half SU(2) fundamentals at one **common gauge vertex**. The first four spins A=(0,1,2,3) form a familiar two-dimensional total-spin-zero register; four others D=(4,5,6,7) form a fixed spin-zero spectator state. Encode one qubit in the two singlet states of A, tensored with a specified reference singlet in D. This is a strict engineered one-vertex model: the original two-vertex four-link matter/rishon graph does **not** directly supply this eight-spin inventory or coupler topology.

The allowed **star** exchanges are (0,4), (0,5), (0,6), (0,7). They act on physical spin A0 and D, but never on physical A1, A2 or A3. The *unwanted* off-graph exchange is (1,4). Every exchange is the Heisenberg scalar

\[
h_{ij}=\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j=(\operatorname{SWAP}_{ij}-I/2)/2,
\]

and commutes with the total local SU(2) generators. Off-graph does **not** mean gauge violating.

A 2024 primary-source simulator proposal, *Spin Exchange-Enabled Quantum Simulator for Large-Scale Non-Abelian Gauge Theories*, **PRX Quantum 5**, 030358 (DOI [10.1103/PRXQuantum.5.030358](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.5.030358)), uses rishons and intravertex Heisenberg exchange to impose gauge constraints. It provides motivation to audit spin-exchange errors, **not** evidence that an artificial star-only graph can be realized: its energetically enforced Gauss law can involve many local spin-exchange couplings. A true platform design must first justify why nonstar interactions are negligible.

## 2. Exact algebra: a singular protection boundary

Within the full fourteen-dimensional eight-spin total-singlet sector, the star-generated exchange algebra has decomposition

\[
\mathcal A_{\star}\simeq M_5(\mathbb C)\otimes I_2\ \oplus\ M_4(\mathbb C),
\]

with complex commutant dimension **5**. Its multiplicity-two sector protects a qubit from arbitrary products of *the specified star exchanges*. This is a standard noiseless-subsystem mechanism, not a newly discovered quantum code.

Adding the independent exchange (1,4) enlarges the connected support to six spins, with two untouched spectator spins (2,3). The six-spin exchange operators generate the symmetric group \(S_6\); matching the two spectator-spin irreducible sectors inside the global singlet gives

\[
\mathcal A_{\star+\mathrm{off}}\simeq M_5(\mathbb C)\oplus M_9(\mathbb C).
\]

Its commutant has dimension **2**, and neither summand has multiplicity greater than one. Therefore **no nontrivial noiseless subsystem is protected against arbitrary errors from this complete generated algebra**. The companion script checks the commutant dimensions **5 → 2** using exact 14-by-14 rational representations, and independently checks six-spin Casimir eigenvalue multiplicities **5** (spin zero) and **9** (spin one).

This change is **algebraically discontinuous** for any nonzero coupling *if* both exchanges are independently allowed as noise generators, but does not imply a discontinuity in finite-time error probability. The algebra of a *single fixed sum Hamiltonian* is not automatically the full algebra generated by its individual terms; that distinction must be preserved.

## 3. Exact finite-size Knill–Laflamme diagnostic

Let \(P\) project onto the two-dimensional A singlet qubit tensored with the fixed D singlet. Let A singlet basis use the code's usual valence-bond convention. The exact mixed overlap of a star edge and a nonstar edge is

\[
P h_{04}h_{14}P=
\begin{pmatrix}-3/16&0\\0&1/16\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The scalar part is \(-I/16\), leaving traceless part \(\operatorname{diag}(-1/8,1/8)\). Thus, for the diagnostic error operators \(h_{04}\) and \(\epsilon h_{14}\),

\[
\left\|P h_{04}(\epsilon h_{14})P
 -\frac12\operatorname{tr}\big(P h_{04}(\epsilon h_{14})P\big)I_2\right\|_{\rm op}
=\frac{|\epsilon|}{8}.
\]

At any nonzero \(\epsilon\), the uncorrected Knill–Laflamme scalarity condition fails for that declared linear error space. This **operator-norm diagnostic is not itself a probability or a universal bound on optimal recovered infidelity**, because physical Kraus normalization and rates matter.

## 4. The finite-amplitude channel

Choose a transparent but uncalibrated set of dimensionless *star* exchange strengths:

\[
(J_{04},J_{05},J_{06},J_{07})=(1,0.6,0.25,-0.15).
\]

Define \(H_\star=\sum_bJ_{0b}h_{0b}\). Model a run-to-run *uncertain* off-graph exchange as a classical equally weighted sign fluctuation in its coefficient:

\[
\mathcal N_{\epsilon,t}(\rho)=\tfrac12 U_+\rho U_+^\dagger+
\tfrac12 U_-\rho U_-^\dagger,\qquad
U_\pm=\exp[-it(H_\star\pm\epsilon h_{14})].
\]

This is a valid completely positive, trace-preserving (CPTP) noise channel. The unknown sign makes it physically meaningful to test information retention, unlike a single perfectly known unitary that could always be inverted. There is **no inferred physical energy scale, dimensional coupling, time in seconds, temperature, noise spectrum, apparatus, or decoherence lifetime**. The evolution is computed on all **256 spin configurations**, not just the four-dimensional original product-singlet block. All operators preserve total Gauss charge, although evolution may leave the chosen encoded subspace. As an independent consistency check, the script also propagates one representative case in a 14D orthonormalized global-singlet basis; the 256D and 14D evolved codewords agree to 2.42 × 10⁻¹⁴ in Euclidean norm.

## 5. Actual recovery: correctable leakage versus logical infidelity

The star fault support is the five-spin set E={A0,D0,D1,D2,D3}. Encode information in the entire two-dimensional total-spin-zero subspace of A while fixing D. The complementary, untouched three spins B={A1,A2,A3} suffice for an **erasure recovery** even after arbitrary unknown operations on E. This follows because a single spin of a four-spin total-singlet register carries no logical information.

The executable constructs an explicit isometry \(K: \mathbb C^2\to(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes8}\), takes the partial trace over E, and applies a completely positive trace-preserving recovery:

\[
\mathcal R(X)= 2K_A^\dagger\big(I_{A0}\otimes X\big)K_A
 +\big(\operatorname{tr}X-
   \operatorname{tr}[2K_A^\dagger(I_{A0}\otimes X)K_A]\big)\frac{I_2}{2}.
\]

Here \(K_A\) embeds the two-dimensional singlet into four A spins; the support projector of \(\operatorname{tr}_{A0}(K_AK_A^\dagger)\) is rank four, with nonzero eigenvalues exactly \(1/2\). The first term is the transpose/Petz recovery for the erasure channel; the second positively completes it outside the support. The program checks the support is a projector, verifies positivity of its complement, and tests all four logical matrix units \(|i\rangle\langle j|\) for exact recovery at zero fault.

**Critical physical limitation:** This recovery assumes discarding five colored physical spins and implementing the abstract transpose recovery on three spins. It is not yet a demonstrated **gauge-compatible** preparation, syndrome extraction, recovery circuit or reset protocol. Consequently the benchmark tests mathematical recoverability, not experimentally realizable quantum memory.

The reported recovered entanglement fidelity \(F_e\) uses the maximally mixed logical input and this **fixed** star-error recovery. For a CPTP qubit channel, average pure-state fidelity is \(F_{\rm avg}=(2F_e+1)/3\). The 'raw' \(F_e\) is the reference overlap before recovery (retaining the same embedded logical target); 'code leakage' is the average probability to leave the original two-dimensional embedded code, **not** Gauss-law violation or irreversible logical loss.

Here all that 'leaked' amplitude remains globally gauge invariant. At epsilon>0 a nonstar interaction reaches spins required for recovery and begins degrading it.

## 6. What is now established, what remains open

**Established for this explicit finite model:**

- Star exchanges preserve a nontrivial noiseless multiplicity in the 14D total SU(2)-singlet sector.
- One independent offgraph exchange collapses that complete-algebra multiplicity, with an exact mixed Knill–Laflamme diagnostic \(|\epsilon|/8\).
- A concrete CPTP erasure recovery exactly reverses arbitrary star-only evolution for the selected 2D inner code, including correctable leakage.
- Under a specified coherent sign-fluctuation channel, recovered infidelity increases approximately as \(\epsilon^2\) for small amplitudes; gauge-law violation remains zero.

**Not established:** whether the star-only coupling restriction occurs naturally or can be engineered in a realistic quantum-link array, whether this recovery can be realized entirely through gauge-compatible operations, whether real noise has this time correlation/distribution, whether it is hardware-advantageous to use this code rather than standard quantum memories, or any connection to physically realized Standard Model anomaly charges/flavor mixing.

**Next engineering decisions:**

1. Map a *specific* simulator's matter and rishon modes and admissible same-vertex couplers onto the 8-spin model. Decide whether its native interactions support or contradict the star assumption.
2. Substitute measured/proposed distributions for parasitic exchange amplitudes and include noisy recovery, preparation and readout. Compare optimal recovery to the explicit fixed erasure recovery instead of equating low projection with logical failure.
3. Test structured control: correlated coupler drift, finite waveform switching, multi-vertex gauge-dressed transfers and gate-mediated multi-block faults.
